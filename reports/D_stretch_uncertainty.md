# Stretch goal: prediction intervals (team teamdev)

Generated by `src/predict.py`. Files: `submission/team_teamdev_submission.csv` (the graded file: row_id, predicted_trips) and `submission/team_teamdev_prediction_intervals.csv` (the same forecast with 80% and 90% ranges and drivers needed).

## Method

Split-conformal intervals on scaled residuals. For each of the two latest 14-day folds, the final model settings are trained on everything before the fold and the errors are divided by sqrt(forecast + 1), because count errors grow with the size of the forecast. The 5/10/90/95% quantiles of these scaled errors give the range: forecast + quantile x sqrt(forecast + 1), floored at 0.

## Does it hold? (honest check)

Quantiles taken from the 4-17 Oct fold only, coverage measured on 18-31 Oct (data the quantiles never saw):

| interval   |   target_coverage |   coverage_18_31_oct |   mean_width_trips |
|:-----------|------------------:|---------------------:|-------------------:|
| 80%        |             0.800 |                0.798 |             16.541 |
| 90%        |             0.900 |                0.896 |             21.829 |

The 80% range caught 80% of actual hours and the 90% range 90%, so the ranges are close to their stated level. The final ranges pool both folds: 80% = forecast -1.42 / +1.65 x sqrt(forecast + 1); 90% = -1.84 / +2.24 x sqrt(forecast + 1). The upper side is wider than the lower side: surges (holiday eves, big events) are under-forecast more often than quiet hours are over-forecast (D7).

## How operations should use it

- Plan drivers from the forecast (`drivers_needed` = forecast / 1.3, rounded up).
- For hours where running short is costly (evening peak, event windows, holiday eves) staff to `drivers_needed_upper_80`: demand should exceed that only about 1 hour in 10.
- The ranges assume the next fortnight behaves like October. A holiday eve or an event that is not in the calendar can still break them (D7).
